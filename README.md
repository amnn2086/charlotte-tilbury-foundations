# charlotte tilbury foundation: how to pick the right formula, get your shade right the first time, and pay less for it

Charlotte Tilbury sells four things it calls foundation, and the differences between them decide whether you end up with a base you wear daily or a bottle that sits untouched because it breaks apart on your nose by lunchtime. The brand name on the lid tells you nothing. The formula inside does.

Here's what each one actually is, who it suits, what it costs, and where the money-saving angle genuinely exists — plus where it doesn't.

## Which Charlotte Tilbury foundations are still available

The line-up is smaller than the internet makes it look. Magic Foundation, the old medium-coverage demi-matte one, has been discontinued — Charlotte Tilbury's own FAQ points Beautiful Skin Foundation at anyone who still wants that kind of finish. Light Wonder Foundation is being phased out too; it has been down to a handful of shades while stock shrinks, and beauty editors at Marie Claire UK and reviewers at Mamabella have both flagged it as on its way out. Treat it as a clearance item rather than a range you can rely on for a repeat purchase.

What's actually on the shelf:

- **Airbrush Flawless Foundation** — full coverage, natural-matte, sold in 44 shades
- **Charlotte's Beautiful Skin Foundation** — medium, buildable, radiant, 30 shades
- **Unreal Skin Sheer Glow Tint** — a sheer-to-light foundation stick, 20 shades
- **Hollywood Flawless Filter** — technically a complexion booster, but Charlotte Tilbury lists it inside the foundation line-up because you can wear it alone, under foundation, mixed into it, or as highlighter

That last point matters more than it sounds. If you search for a "Charlotte Tilbury foundation" and end up on the Flawless Filter page, you haven't been misdirected — you've just landed on the sheerest end of the range.

## Airbrush Flawless Foundation: the one everyone means

This is the reformulated version, and it's the reason "charlotte tilbury foundation" as a search term is dominated by matte, full-coverage reviews. The current formula is billed as a 7-in-1, non-comedogenic, 24-hour base with three named technologies: Powder Blur AIRTech for the blur, Biomimetic Second-Skin Tech for wear, and Phytoyouth Essence, which the brand frames as the skincare half of the hybrid. The brand's own testing on 305 people over eight weeks reports 97% agreeing it covered the look of pigmentation, dark spots, blemishes and scarring, and 90% reporting a 24-hour colour match. Those are brand-run numbers, so read them as marketing with a study attached, not independent evidence.

What reviewers report is more useful. Marie Claire's tester, who normally prefers light coverage, found the new texture less matte than expected, closer to medium than full on her first pass, and self-setting enough that she didn't need much powder — with a little rub-off on the nose by evening. Refinery29's editor praised how thoroughly one pump blurred pores and leftover acne marks, and flagged that a single pump covers the whole face. On drier skin, the picture changes: Mamabella's reviewers found it the thickest formula in the range, quick to set, and prone to settling in enlarged pores up close, while Space NK's team noted it takes real blending effort to work in.

The brand's own application guidance is worth following here: half a pump for a light, "clean girl" finish, one full pump for full coverage. Most people who find this foundation cakey are using three times that much.

## Charlotte's Beautiful Skin Foundation: the glow default

If your skin leans dry, or you're past the point of wanting matte anything, this is the one Charlotte Tilbury itself points to. Medium coverage you build up, a radiant finish, and a formula built around hyaluronic acid, a brightening rose complex and Collageneer, with the brand claiming up to 24 hours of hydration and 16 hours of wear.

The honest caveat, and it's a recurring one in reviews: radiant is genuinely radiant. Marie Claire's tester with oily skin described looking shiny rather than glowing by lunchtime until she started setting her T-zone with powder. So if you're buying this for oily skin, budget for a setting powder in the same order. If you're on the dry or mature side, it's the least effortful of the four — reviewers consistently note it doesn't settle into fine lines the way the matte formula can.

## Unreal Skin Sheer Glow Tint: coverage for people who don't want coverage

A hydrating stick in 0.3 oz format, 20 shades, sheer-to-light and buildable. Hyaluronic acid, vitamin E and Collageneer do the hydration work; it's vegan and cruelty-free. Charlotte Tilbury's marketing calls it a "no-foundation foundation," which is accurate enough — it evens tone and adds a soft-focus glow, but it will not hide pigmentation, active breakouts or scarring.

Where it earns its place is the speed. You swipe, you blend, you're out. If you've tried the full-coverage formula on a rushed weekday morning and hated the result, this is the same brand solving a different problem.

## Hollywood Flawless Filter: read the label before you buy

Worth stating plainly, because it catches people out: this is a complexion booster with light coverage, not a foundation in the traditional sense. It blurs, smooths and reflects light back off the skin, and Sephora's listing notes it can be worn as a tinted primer, under foundation, mixed into foundation, or as a highlighter. Used alone, it dials redness down a notch and leaves a dewy sheen — Marie Claire's tester described a slight metallic cast if you overdo it. Used over a full-coverage base, you get the opposite of what you wanted.

If you want the Flawless Filter look and actual coverage, the Unreal Skin stick or Beautiful Skin Foundation is the better spend.

## Getting the shade right, which is the part that actually goes wrong

The four products don't share a shade numbering system in a way you can rely on. Mamabella's reviewers put it directly: a 2 Neutral exists in the Airbrush range but not in Beautiful Skin, so knowing your shade in one product doesn't guarantee the match in another. Charlotte Tilbury publishes a Complexion Matrix to translate between ranges — use it, or use the shade finder on the product page.

The practical rules:

1. Undertone first, depth second. Cool undertones suit blue or purple wrist veins and silver jewellery; warm suits green veins and gold; neutral sits between. The brand splits all four ranges across cool, neutral and warm.
2. Avoid buying two bottles at once. Charlotte Tilbury advertises a free shade exchange on its own site in some markets — the UK offer lets you swap once at no cost, standard shipping included. Retailers like Sephora and Ulta have their own return windows, but opened cosmetics are treated differently, so check before you commit.
3. If you're between shades in Airbrush, go half a step lighter on oily skin and warmer-neutral on dry skin. Matte formulas with 44 shades rarely run as forgiving as radiant ones.

## Application details that decide whether you like it

These come from the brand's own instructions and reviewers who used the products properly:

- Airbrush goes on best with a damp sponge or a dense brush, buffed from the centre of the face outward. Apply and blend quickly, because it sets fast.
- Powder before the setting spray, not instead of it, if you're oily. The Airbrush setting spray is designed to fuse the layers.
- Beautiful Skin Foundation doesn't need a heavy primer. It does need a light setting powder in the T-zone if your skin is combination or oily.
- Flawless Filter over the high points of the cheeks reads as a highlighter; all over under foundation reads as a glow primer. Pick one role per use.

## Questions people ask before ordering

**Is Airbrush Flawless Foundation worth the money?** It's the most convincing full-coverage option in the range and one pump genuinely does a whole face, which stretches the value. It's a poor fit if your skin is dry or heavily textured — the formula will find every flake.

**Which Charlotte Tilbury foundation is best for mature skin?** Reviewers consistently steer mature skin toward Beautiful Skin Foundation and away from Airbrush, mainly because the matte formula settles into fine lines while the radiant one floats. One Marie Claire tester's 56-year-old mother specifically called out the reformulated Airbrush staying put over her lines without settling, so it isn't absolute — but Beautiful Skin remains the safer first buy.

**Does the stick last as long as the liquid?** No. A sheer tint stick is a shorter-wear product by design. If you need a base that survives a 12-hour day, that's the Airbrush or Beautiful Skin job.

**Can I mix Flawless Filter with foundation?** Yes, and it's an intended use. Add a small amount to a medium-coverage base rather than layering a full pump on top.

## The short version

Airbrush Flawless Foundation if you want full coverage, a blurred matte finish and skin that leans oily or balanced. Beautiful Skin Foundation if you're dry, mature or just want glow with less effort. The Unreal Skin stick for fast everyday coverage. Hollywood Flawless Filter if what you actually want is light and sheen, not coverage — and be honest with yourself about that before checkout.

Buy it when a retailer promotion lands, protect the tracking if you're going through cashback, and don't buy two bottles at once in a shade you haven't worn.
